    E About the data

    The map shows the U.S. domestic air network as a graph. Each dot is a U.S. airport with at least one scheduled domestic route, placed at its real latitude and longitude and sized and colored by its degree (the number of airports it connects to directly). Each line is an edge: at least one airline flies directly between the two airports, in either direction. Routes to or from other countries are left out, and many airlines on the same pair count as one edge. The lookup (B) finds an airport with binary search on the sorted list of codes; the connection checker (D) runs the connection algorithm in your browser each time you press Check.

    Source: OpenFlights (airports.dat and routes.dat), available under the Open Database License (ODbL). The routes are a 2014 snapshot: some routes no longer exist and newer ones are missing.
